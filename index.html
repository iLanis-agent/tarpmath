Tarp math

A 3x3 tarp pitched at waist height is not a 3-meter bedroom. Ridge height eats floor width through one right triangle - and nobody runs it until the rain starts.

Pitch it on paper first

The problem it solves

Every tarp pitch is the same trade: raise the ridge for headroom and the walls come in, drop it for coverage and you crawl. The floor you get is the base of a right triangle - half the tarp width is the hypotenuse, the ridge height is one leg, and the covered floor is the other. Guessed by eye, a 3x3 A-frame at 1.2 meters of ridge leaves 1.8 meters of floor, not the 3 you packed for.

Tarp math runs the triangle for the three classic pitches - A-frame, lean-to and diamond - plus the guy-line length and angle for a given attachment height and stake distance. Any unit works, as long as you use it everywhere.

What it does not do

This is geometry, not a weather report. Fabric stretch, sag, seam strength, wind load and rain run-off are not modeled, and no output here rates a pitch for a storm. The sleeper labels describe floor width only. Site choice and judgment stay with you.
